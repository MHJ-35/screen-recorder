Video

Quality & resolution
This is the quality you are asking for. The real result depends on your screen. For example, choosing 4K on a 1080p screen will not actually give you 4K detail.
Frame rate
Higher fps looks smoother for fast motion or games, but creates a bigger file. 30fps is a good standard for tutorials and screen recordings.
File format
WebM (VP9/Opus) is what every major browser can reliably record to. MP4 support depends on your browser/OS and is offered only when detected.
Include system/tab audio
Captures sound playing on your screen (a video, app audio) in addition to your mic, if your browser and selected source support it.

Microphone

Device
Click "Add microphone" below once to confirm which mic is active.
Microphone volume
Boost a quiet mic or turn down a hot one.
100%
Recommended: 100% (unchanged) unless your mic sounds noticeably quiet. Pushing past ~150 to 180% amplifies background hiss along with your voice, and can start to distort or clip if you speak loudly. Automatic gain control has been removed, since its constant real time adjustment tended to cause pumping and pick up more background noise. A fixed manual level here gives cleaner, more consistent results.
Sample rate
48kHz is the standard choice and works well for almost everyone. Only switch to 44.1kHz if you have a specific reason to.
Mute microphone
Silences your mic without removing it, so screen audio can still record if enabled above.
Reduce background noise
Browser level noise suppression handles steady sounds like fan hum, AC, and hiss in real time. It covers this on its own, so no separate setting is needed for those.
Echo cancellation
Stops your speaker output (like a call partner's voice) from being picked back up by your mic.
Audio bitrate
Independent of video quality. Raises how much detail is kept in the sound itself, which matters most for music, singing, or anything beyond plain speech.
If you change any setting above, click "Add microphone" again below to apply it.

Safety

If the tab is closed mid-recording
Your browser will first ask you to confirm before the tab actually closes. If you go through with it anyway, this tool makes one best effort attempt to save whatever was captured up to that instant, as a single file, with no repeated checkpoint files. This isn't guaranteed (some browsers block downloads at the moment of closing), but it costs nothing to try.

Compatibility

Works on Chrome, Edge, and Firefox on Windows, Mac, and Linux. This is a single, self contained file, so you can send it to anyone and they open it the same way, with no install or account needed. Not supported on mobile browsers (screen recording from a web page isn't available there, and not at all on iOS Safari).
